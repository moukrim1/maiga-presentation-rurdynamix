## Slide 4 - Study Area (0:45)

**What to say**:

- Population: ~24,700 people
- Altitude averages 1,290 meters — steep terrain!
- 80% average slope (think: mountain goat country)
- Mediterranean mountain climate: cool wet winters, hot dry summers
- Limited water, so farmers depend on seasonal rainfall + groundwater

**Show map**: Point to location on map

**Key takeaway**: This is a marginal agricultural zone pushed to its limits

## Slide 5 - Methodology (1:00)

**What to say**:
- How did we study this?
- Three main data sources:

1. **Landsat satellite images** (since 1998) — free, 30-meter resolution, long archive
2. **Google Earth Pro** — manual basin digitization (very reliable)
3. **Google Earth Engine** — automated NDVI calculations over decades

- **Tools**: QGIS and ArcGIS for mapping; RStudio for statistics

**Key insight**: Remote sensing + GIS lets us track fine landscape changes even in remote mountain areas

## Slide 6 - Basin Growth Results (1:00)

**What to say**:
- Here's the most striking finding: basin explosion

- From dozens to thousands in 15 years
- Growth rate: 20,000% (yes, twenty thousand percent!)
- Or: ~300 new basins per year on average
- BUT — not linear! Slow until 2013, then steep after, nearly vertical post-2021

**Show graph**:
- Flat line before 2013
- Curve bends upward 2013-2021
- Near-vertical slope after legalization

**Remark**: These basins aren't on any official map — it's local innovation, not state planning

## Slide 7 - Location Factors (1:30)

**What to say**:
- Question: Why HERE and not there? Basins aren't random!

**Three main controls**:

**Elevation (600-1,200 m)**:
- Concentration in this band
- Avoids very high peaks (too cold, too hard to build)
- Avoids very low areas (too arid, forest cover)

**Slope (15-35%)**:
- Not flat (water won't accumulate)
- Not too steep (too expensive to build, erosion)
- This angle is "just right" — gravity optimization

**Water access (< 500m to streams/springs)**:
- CRITICAL factor
- If water isn't nearby, no basin feasible
- Valley bottoms strongly favored
- Hydrographic network is destiny

**Take-home**: Farmers aren't stupid — they place basins where physics and economics make sense

---

## Slide 8 - NDVI and Vegetation (1:30)

**What to say**:
- Here's a paradox worth noting

- NDVI = satellite "greenness" index (0 = bare soil, 1 = dense forest)
- 1998-2013: NDVI mostly flat or declining
- 2013-2026: Big jump — vegetation got greener
- **Timing**: This breakpoint right when basins started expanding!

**But caution**:
- Correlation ≠ causation
- Rainfall also increased after 2013 (we checked)
- Could be natural climate cycle
- Could be irrigation + better growing conditions
- Could be both

**Honest conclusion**: We see a link, but can't prove causality

---

## Slide 9 - Basin Characteristics (0:45)

**What to say**:
- Let's look at basin numbers themselves

- Average basin: 2,500 m² (like a 50m × 50m square)
- BUT: Clear miniaturization
- Old basins (2010): often > 10,000 m²
- Recent basins (2024): mostly < 1,000 m²
- Depth: typically 2-4 meters
- Total volume stored: millions of cubic meters region-wide

**Perspective**: That's like filling dozens of Olympic swimming pools worth of water from underground

---

## Slide 10 - Discussion: Is This Sustainable? (1:30)

**What to say**:
- Now the hard questions: Is this durable?

**Four big risks**:

1. **Hydrology**: How much water leaves the system? Groundwater depletion?
2. **Environment**: 5,000+ basins = forest loss, soil disruption, cumulative ecological stress
3. **Social**: Cannabis gets priority water; food crops come second → inequality
4. **Climate**: If rains decrease further (as models suggest), does the whole system collapse?

**Knowledge gap**:
- We see NDVI improved, but we don't know if basins caused it
- We don't know aquifer recharge rates vs extraction
- We don't know long-term resilience under climate stress

## Slide 11 - Recommendations (1:30)

**What to say**:
- Three levels of action needed:

**For research**:
- Hydrologic study: measure actual water extraction, aquifer response
- Climate modeling: what happens to basin strategy under pessimistic rainfall scenarios?
- Talk to farmers: What strategies are they considering? What are their constraints?

**For local management**:
- Stop the spatial chaos; plan irrigation strategically
- Use satellite monitoring as an early warning system
- Set limits on basin density or size

**For policy**:
- Cannabis policy, water policy, and climate policy MUST align
- Current trajectory: productivity vs. durability — choose balance
- Long-term: transition toward genuine sustainability, not just adaptation

## 💡 Presentation Tips

1. **Pacing**: Don't rush. Slides are visual aids, not a script to read
2. **Transitions**: Pause 2-3 seconds between slide groups to reset audience attention
3. **Eye contact**: Find people actively listening; hold their gaze
4. **Passion**: This is human story + science. Show you care about the outcome
5. **Honesty**: Say "we don't know" when true (e.g., basin-NDVI causality). It builds credibility
6. **Visuals**: When showing maps, point them out, give people time to absorb
7. **Ending**: End with the central message, not "Thank you." Leave them with something to remember
