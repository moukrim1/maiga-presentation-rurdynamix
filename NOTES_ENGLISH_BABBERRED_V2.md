## Slide 2 - The Challenge (0:45)

**What to say**:
- The Mediterranean mountain regions face an acute challenge: water scarcity
- In Bab Berred specifically, we observe three converging pressures:

**Precipitation is declining** (not increasing):
- 40-year trend (1985-2024) shows significant negative trend
- Mann-Kendall test: tau = -0.1376 (statistically significant decline)
- Year-to-year variability is extreme: wet years >850 mm, dry years <410 mm
- 2023 hit a record low: only ~300 mm (unprecedented)

**Result**: Agricultural production becomes riskier every year
- Rainfed agriculture increasingly unreliable
- Summer droughts intensify
- Seasonal water stress becomes pronounced

**Why matters**: This declining precipitation is the foundational driver

## Slide 3 - The Real Driver: Economics, Not Law (0:50)

**What to say** (critical clarification):
- It's crucial to understand: What is NOT the main driver here
- Legalization in 2021 (Law 13-21) is NOT the primary cause
- Instead, the primary driver is **international market demand** for cannabis
- This demand has been strong for decades, legalization just enabled official channels

**Why farmers switched to hybrid varieties**:
- Traditional kif variety: ~15 kg/ha of hashish
- Modern hybrid varieties: ~70 kg/ha of hashish
- That's a 4-5× productivity increase!
- But hybrids require intensive irrigation

**The legalization gap**:
- Official authorized cultivation area ≠ actual cultivation area
- Actual area >> authorized area
- Many farmers still cultivate outside official channels
- This gap persists even after 2021 legalization

**Conclusion**: International market demand, not legalization, explains the cannabis transformation

## Slide 4 - Bab Berred: The Study Area (0:50)

**What to say**:
- Bab Berred is a rural mountain municipality in Chefchaouen Province
- 24,742 residents depending primarily on agriculture
- Geographic facts:
  - Total area: 17,224 hectares (172.24 km²)
  - Altitude range: 650-2,100 meters
  - Extreme topography: 80% average slope (nearly vertical in places!)
  - Aspect: south-east facing (intensifies summer heat)

**Climate context**:
- Mean annual precipitation: >700 mm
- BUT highly seasonal: concentrated October-April
- Summer (May-September): Hot, dry stress
- Mean annual temperature: 18.6°C
- June mean: 24.6°C (quite hot for mountain)

**Natural vegetation**:
- Moroccan cedar at high elevations (>1,000 m)
- Mediterranean shrubland at lower elevations
- Recently: conversion to cannabis cultivation

**Key point**: This is a marginal agricultural zone pushed to its limits by water scarcity

## Slide 5 - Basin Explosion: The Numbers (1:00)

**What to say**:
- Here's the most striking finding from our remote sensing analysis:

**Basin inventory 2010-2025:**
- 2010: Approximately 28 artificial basins
- 2025: 2,044 basins
- Overall density: 11.87 basins per km²
- Mean spacing: approximately 290 meters between basins

**Growth pattern** (not uniform):
- 2010-2014: Gradual growth
- 2015-2021: Accelerating construction
- 2022-2025: Intense investment, 372 basins in 2024-2025 alone

**Hydrological transformation:**
- 2010 total storage: ~50,000 m³
- 2025 total storage: ~4.3 million m³
- Overall increase: 85-fold expansion in just 15 years

**Implication**: Massive hydrological alteration of the territory

---

## Slide 6 - Basin Morphometrics: Miniaturization (0:50)

**What to say**:
- Interestingly, the basins are getting smaller over time

**Size trend:**
- 2010 average basin: 1,260 m²
- 2025 average basin: 812 m²
- By 2025: 90% of basins < 1,500 m²

**Depth and volume:**
- Mean depth: 5.5 meters
- Mean storage volume per basin: ~2,100 m³

**Why miniaturization matters:**
- Reflects economic constraints in rural communities
- Farmers building incrementally with limited capital
- Multiple small reservoirs instead of fewer large ones
- Pragmatic adaptation to resource scarcity

**What this reveals**: Communities are building what they can afford, not what they ideally want

## Slide 7 - Where Are Basins Located? (1:00)

**What to say**:
- Basin locations are NOT random
- Three clear geographic patterns control placement:

**Altitude preference (800-1,500 m):**
- Not at very high elevations (too difficult, cold, expensive)
- Not at very low elevations (too arid, far from water sources)
- Sweet spot reflects balance between water demand intensity and water source proximity

**Slope preference (71% on 5-15% slopes):**
- Virtually absent on slopes >25%
- Reflects engineering constraints
- Gravity-fed systems work best on moderate slopes
- Steep slopes = more expensive to build

**Land-use concentration (78% in cultivated lands):**
- 18% in grasslands
- Only 3% in natural vegetation areas
- Shows deliberate agricultural intensification
- Basins placed where cannabis is grown

**Interpretation**: Farmers respond rationally to biophysical constraints

## Slide 8 - The Paradox Part 1: Precipitation Decline (1:00)

**What to say**:
- Here's the fundamental problem setting the stage for basins

**Long-term precipitation trend (1985-2024):**
- Clear progressive decline over 40 years
- Not just random variability—systematic downward trend
- Mann-Kendall test confirms: tau = -0.1376 (p < 0.05, statistically significant)
- This is climate change signal arriving in real time

**But extreme variability hides the trend:**
- Very wet years: 1996, 2010, 2018 (>850 mm)
- Very dry years: 1995, 1998, 2005, 2015, 2020 (<410 mm)
- Year-to-year swings make planning nearly impossible
- **2023 record**: Lowest precipitation ever recorded (~300 mm)

**Agricultural consequence:**
- Rainfed agriculture becomes increasingly risky
- Farmers need water storage for drought years
- Motivation for artificial basin construction as adaptation

**Key insight**: Basin proliferation is a rational response to declining water availability

## Slide 9 - The Paradox Part 2: NDVI Improvement (1:00)

**What to say**:
- Here's the most striking finding—seemingly contradictory:

**NDVI vegetation analysis (March-July, 1998-2025):**
- 1998-2012: Slight improvement (Sen's slope 0.0025 per year)
- 2012 onward: Dramatic acceleration
- Statistical break detected in 2012 (p = 0.00000434, very significant)
- Peak amplitudes: Highest during cannabis growing season (May-August)

**The paradox stated clearly:**
- **Precipitation is declining** overall (bad for vegetation)
- **NDVI is improving strongly** (good for vegetation)
- **Both trends shift around 2012-2013**

**What this means:**
- Irrigation infrastructure is compensating for natural water deficit
- Artificial basins enable continued vegetation productivity despite drier climate
- But read the next slide for what this REALLY represents...

## Slide 10 - The Deception: What NDVI Really Shows (1:00)

**What to say**:
- Here's the critical insight: NDVI improvement masks ecological reality

**What NDVI indicates:**
- Biomass productivity (amount of green stuff)
- NOT ecosystem health
- NOT biodiversity status
- NOT ecological improvement

**Where the signal actually comes from:**
- Irrigated cannabis monoculture
- Cannabis achieves NDVI > 0.75 (among highest of any crop globally)
- Dense canopy during peak growth (March-July)
- Our measurement period perfectly captures this peak
- High biomass signal is real, but misleading

**What ACTUALLY happened to the landscape:**
- Natural areas (grassland, rangeland): **declined**
- Cultivated agricultural areas: **expanded**
- Land-use transformation: Forest/grassland → intensive cannabis
- NOT ecosystem-wide improvement; **ecosystem conversion**!

**The critical message:**
- Remote sensing vegetation indices alone suggest environmental improvement
- But integrated analysis (NDVI + precipitation + land-use) reveals true story
- This is not ecological improvement; it's agricultural intensification masquerading as green recovery

---

## Slide 11 - The Hydrological Crisis (1:00)

**What to say**:
- Now we come to the core sustainability challenge

**The fundamental problem:**
- Water extraction from basins and irrigation **exceeds annual renewable recharge capacity**
- Groundwater is being depleted faster than it replenishes
- Progressive piezometric (water table) decline is occurring
- Long-term water security is being undermined

**Why this is critical:**
- Aquifer depletion is irreversible on human timescales
- Precipitation deficits intensifying with climate change
- 2,000+ small basins create complex cumulative pressure on hydrology
- May trigger unintended ecosystem consequences

**The adaptation paradox:**
- Short-term: Infrastructure enables high productivity
- Long-term: May cause water insecurity worse than original scarcity
- Individual farmers rationally building basins
- Collectively: Unsustainable aquifer depletion

**Hard truth**: Current strategy solves short-term water crisis but may create long-term water catastrophe

## Slide 12 - Ecosystem Fragmentation (1:00)

**What to say**:
- Beyond hydrology, there are severe ecological consequences

**Landscape fragmentation:**
- Natural spaces increasingly fragmented by basin infrastructure
- Cannabis cultivation expanding through grasslands
- Disrupts wildlife habitat connectivity
- Reduces sanctuary area for endemic species

**Biodiversity at risk in Rif Mountains:**
- Rif harbors important endemic plant and animal species
- Several species exist only in restricted refuge areas
- These refugia are within or adjacent to Bab Berred
- Habitat fragmentation directly threatens species persistence
- Ecosystem functionality becomes compromised

**What remote sensing shows:**
- Land-use change maps reveal forest loss
- Figure 10 shows 2006 vs 2023 comparison (dramatic transformation)
- Natural forest and grassland converted to agricultural use
- Visible infrastructure (roads, cleared areas) for basin access

**Ecological summary:**
- Apparent NDVI improvement masks simultaneous ecological deterioration
- Biodiversity loss not captured by vegetation indices
- Need integrated indicators, not just greenness

## Slide 13 - Governance Challenges (1:00)

**What to say**:
- These findings point directly to governance failures

**Current situation (the tragedy of the commons):**
- Absence of coordinated water management
- Each farmer individually optimizes household advantage
- Individually rational decisions ≠ collectively sustainable outcomes
- Result: Aquifer depletion, landscape fragmentation, biodiversity loss

**What needs to change:**
1. **Hydrological assessments** imposing sustainable extraction ceilings
2. **Natural habitat protection** preventing further conversion
3. **Agricultural diversification** reducing cannabis monoculture risk
4. **Water efficiency improvements** (drip irrigation, etc.)
5. **Land-use planning** integrating conservation with agriculture

**Role of remote sensing in governance:**
- NDVI alone suggests everything is improving
- Integrated analysis (NDVI + precipitation + land-use + hydrology) reveals challenges
- Science is essential for evidence-based policy

**Global relevance:**
- Same pattern repeated across Mediterranean, Central Asia, Middle East
- Infrastructure-intensive agricultural transformation worldwide
- Many regions likely experiencing similar ecological decline masked by NDVI
- Need for integrated monitoring approaches globally

## Slide 14 - Conclusion (0:45)

**What to say**:
- Let me summarize the key findings and their significance

**What we documented in Bab Berred:**
- Rapid artificial basin proliferation (28 → 2,044 basins, 2010-2025)
- Infrastructure compensating for declining precipitation (trend: -0.1376/year)
- Apparent vegetation improvement (NDVI up 2012+) masking ecological degradation
- Aquifer overexploitation threatening long-term water security

**Why this matters beyond Bab Berred:**
- Same pattern replicated across Mediterranean mountains
- Semi-arid regions worldwide adopting similar infrastructure
- Remote sensing-based monitoring often misses underlying ecological decline
- Governance frameworks must transcend infrastructure investment alone

**The fundamental challenge:**
- Sustainable rural futures require integrated governance
- Must simultaneously address:
  - Hydrological limits (aquifer capacity)
  - Ecosystem integrity (biodiversity, habitat)
  - Livelihood resilience (farmer income security)
- Particularly urgent as climate change intensifies water scarcity

**Final message**:
- Infrastructure innovation is not wrong; it's necessary
- But cannot substitute for integrated water resource management
- Must couple infrastructure with ecosystem protection and livelihood diversification
- This case study from Morocco reflects global rural transformation challenges
