**Abstract**

In the central Moroccan Rif, the expansion of cannabis cultivation is
accompanied by a remarkable proliferation of artificial basins for
irrigation water storage. This study analyzes, using remote sensing and
GIS, their spatio-temporal dynamics between 2010 and 2025 in Bab Berred
rural municipality, Chefchaouen province. Results show a considerable
increase in basin numbers from the early 2010s, rising from a few basins
to over 2,000 structures (representing a density of 11.87 basins/km²
across 172.24 km²), with marked acceleration in recent years. Basin
spatial distribution depends on land use, topography, reflecting
adaptation to environmental constraints rather than centralized
planning. A paradoxical finding emerges: whereas annual precipitation
shows a general declining trend throughout the study period,
NDVI\_~March-Jul~ reveals significant and sustained improvement in
vegetation cover, with a marked break in 2012 coinciding closely with
the period of artificial basin proliferation. This NDVI\_~March-Jul~
improvement persists and intensifies, suggesting that infrastructural
irrigation substantially compensates for the hydro-climatic deficit and
reflects intensification of irrigated monoculture rather than genuine
ecological improvement. These results reveal a territorially effective
short-term adaptation strategy that masks critical sustainability
challenges: aquifer overexploitation, landscape fragmentation,
transformation of natural ecosystems, and loss of Mediterranean
biodiversity. These dynamics call for integrated territorial governance
where infrastructural adaptation alone cannot substitute for sustainable
water resource management.

1\. Introduction

Rural territories across the Mediterranean region currently experience
profound transformations driven by climate change, economic pressures,
and technological innovation \[1\]. These transitions challenge
traditional land management systems and force communities to adopt new
adaptation strategies. The central Moroccan Rif Mountains exemplify
these contemporary rural dynamics, where cannabis cultivation has
undergone significant transformation in recent decades.

Historically, the Rif region produced cannabis through cultivation of
kif, a local landrace variety that evolved over centuries to suit the
region's challenging environmental conditions: irregular precipitation,
poor soils, and limited water availability. Kif cultivation enabled
rural communities to sustain agriculture in a geographically marginal
region unsuitable for conventional crops, utilizing rainfed agriculture
and requiring minimal external inputs. This traditional approach
represented a rational adaptation to local environmental constraints,
enabling rural communities to maintain agricultural livelihoods in a
geographically marginal region despite historical legal restrictions on
cannabis cultivation.

Since the early 2010s, however, the introduction and rapid expansion of
modern hybrid cannabis varieties has fundamentally altered cultivation
practices and territorial dynamics \[2\]. These hybridized varieties,
selected for high yields and responding to strong international market
demand, require substantially greater water availability than the
traditional variety \[3\]. The productivity difference is significant:
local variety produces approximately 15 kilograms of hashish per hectare
through traditional sieving methods, while modern hybrid varieties yield
approximately 70 kilograms per hectare of high-quality hashish \[4\].
This 4-to-5 fold productivity increase explains rapid farmer adoption,
despite the requirement for intensive irrigation.

The expansion of cannabis cultivation in the Rif occurs within a complex
regulatory context. Morocco legalized cannabis cultivation for medical
and industrial purposes in 2021 (Law 13-21), yet the actual cultivated
area substantially exceeds the officially authorized area. This
semi-legal situation persists despite legalization, with farmers
continuing cultivation within and outside official channels. The primary
driver of this expansion has been sustained international market demand
rather than legalization itself \[3\]. The legalization framework has
enabled expansion in certain zones where farmers obtained official
authorizations, but the gap between authorized and actual cultivated
areas remains substantial, indicating that international demand has been
the dominant factor shaping cultivation dynamics.

Bab Berred, a rural commune in the Central Rif region of Chefchaouen
Province, illustrates these dynamics with particular intensity. Its
population depends primarily on agriculture, historically relying on
rain-fed cultivation of cereals and cannabis, as well as extensive
livestock farming. Since the early 2010s, however, the landscape in this
rural municipality has undergone a dramatic transformation due to the
expansion of the cultivation of new cannabis varieties and the growth of
artificial irrigation infrastructure.

Water scarcity constitutes a defining constraint on agricultural
production in Mediterranean mountain regions. Climate projections
indicate increasing aridity across the Mediterranean basin, with
precipitation decline already observable in mountain areas including the
Rif \[5--7\]. Summer droughts intensify, creating pronounced seasonal
water stress for rainfed agriculture. Within this hydrological context
of increasing precipitation deficit, rural communities have increasingly
deployed artificial water basins as adaptive infrastructure enabling
agricultural intensification and water security during drought periods.
Between 2010 and 2025, basin construction accelerated dramatically in
Bab Berred and other Rif municipalities \[8\], transforming the physical
landscape, water management regimes, and agricultural production
systems.

Yet this adaptation strategy raises critical questions about
sustainability and long-term territorial resilience. Rapid basin
proliferation may exceed groundwater recharge capacity, creating aquifer
depletion dynamics that undermine water security beyond the short term
\[9\]. Landscape transformation accompanying irrigation infrastructure
development and crop intensification potentially threatens endemic
species habitats and ecosystem integrity. Furthermore, concentration of
agricultural production on cannabis monoculture creates economic
vulnerability to international mutation (control, legalization, ...),
price fluctuation or regulatory change. The apparent vegetation
productivity gains associated with irrigation mask underlying ecological
deterioration.

Understanding these dynamics requires integrating spatial analysis with
climate data and land-use monitoring. Remote sensing technologies enable
quantitative assessment of how basin proliferation correlates with
agricultural expansion and vegetation productivity changes, illuminating
mechanisms linking infrastructure investment, land transformation, and
territorial change. Such analysis proves essential for designing
governance frameworks that reconcile agricultural development with
environmental sustainability in vulnerable Mediterranean mountain
territories undergoing rapid socio-economic transition.

2\. Material and Methods

2.1. Study Area: Bab Berred Municipality

Bab Berred is a rural municipality in central Rif Mountains, northern
Morocco (35°00\' - 4°58\'). The municipality encompasses 17,224 hectares
spanning altitudes from 650 to 2,100 meters, characterized by steep
slopes averaging 80 percent gradient and south-east aspect orientation.
Its 24,742 residents depend predominantly on agriculture and economic
conditions are constrained. This municipality experiences Mediterranean
mountain climate with mean annual precipitation exceeding 700
millimeters, concentrated during winter months (October to April).
Summers are hot and dry (May to September), with mean June temperature
of 24.6 degrees Celsius, creating pronounced seasonal water stress. Mean
annual temperature averages 18.6 °C. Natural vegetation comprises mixed
forest-grassland mosaic. Moroccan cedar forests occupy higher elevations
(greater than 1,000 m), while Mediterranean shrubland dominates lower
elevations. The municipality harbors Pyrenean oak (*Quercus pyrenaica*),
a distinctive Mediterranean emblematic species. However, recent decades
witnessed substantial natural area regression replaced by cannabis
cultivation and agricultural development, reflecting economic pressures
toward agricultural intensification.

2.2. Data Sources, Processing and Analytical Methods

Satellite imagery analysis employed Landsat 5, 7, and 8 data (30-meter
resolution) accessed through Google Earth Engine platform \[10\] for the
period 1998 to 2025, enabling vegetation index calculation as a
vegetation proxy. Historical imagery from Google Earth Pro and Esri
World Imagery Wayback archive enabled manual artificial basin
digitization and validation through systematic visual interpretation.
Climate data (annual precipitation 1998 to 2025) derived from satellite
precipitation products. Land-cover classification employed ESA
WorldCover 2021 product, identifying seven classes present in Bab
Berred: tree cover, shrubland, grassland, cultivated areas, built-up
zones, sparse vegetation and bare soil, and water bodies.

Spatio-temporal analysis examined basin count, density, and distribution
patterns across the 2010 to 2025 period within Bab Berred. Spatial
distribution analysis evaluated basin location relative to topographic
variables (altitude, slope) and land-use categories. Morphometric
analysis characterized basin properties: surface area, estimated depth,
and storage volume. Vegetation index time series (1998 to 2025) were
calculated from Landsat surface reflectance data and analyzed for
temporal trends and inflection points \[11,12\]. To limit seasonal
variability effects and focus on the primary growth period for cannabis
cultivation in the study area, vegetation index analysis was restricted
to imagery acquired between March and July for each year. Monthly
vegetation index averages were computed to track seasonal vegetation
variations, while annual averages for the March-July period were
calculated for each year to characterize mean vegetation condition
during cannabis peak growth and account for interannual seasonal
variability. Annual precipitation time series underwent linear trend
analysis. Spatial analysis employed QGIS platforms for vector and raster
processing.

3\. Results and Discussion

3.1. Artificial Basin Proliferation in Bab Berred

Basin inventory for Bab Berred revealed remarkable proliferation of
artificial basins, increasing from approximately 28 structures in 2010
to a cumulative total of 2,044 basins by 2025. This corresponds to an
overall basin density of 11.87 structures/km2. This equates to mean
spacing of approximately 290 meters between basins. Growth remained
gradual through 2012 to 2014, then accelerated substantially from 2015
onward, with particularly intense construction in 2022 to 2025. The
period 2024-2025 recorded 372 newly constructed basins, indicating
sustained infrastructure investment.

Morphometric analysis revealed basin characteristics reflecting
construction pragmatism and resource constraints. Mean basin size
declined from 1,260 m² (2010) to 812 m² (2025), indicating a strategic
shift toward numerous small-scale storage units rather than fewer large
reservoirs. This miniaturization pattern reflects economically
constrained rural communities building water storage infrastructure
incrementally with limited financial resources, constructing multiple
modest basins rather than fewer large structures requiring substantial
capital investment.

By 2025, 90 percent of basins occupy surface area less than 1,500 m2,
with mean estimated depth 5.5 m and mean storage volume approximately
2,100 m3 per basin. Total water storage capacity expanded from
approximately 50,000 m3 (2010) to approximately 4.3 million m3 (2025)
with an 85-fold increase representing massive hydrological
transformation that fundamentally altered the territory's water
availability and agricultural productivity.

3.2. Spatial and Sustainability Controls on Basin Development

Basin spatial distribution throughout Bab Berred reflected adaptation to
biophysical constraints and land-parcel organization rather than planned
territorial organization. Altitude analysis revealed basin concentration
between 800 to 1,500 meters elevation, reflecting optimal balance
between irrigation demand intensity and water source proximity. Slope
preference was pronounced: 71 % of basins occupied slopes between 5 to
15 %, with virtually no basins on slopes exceeding 25 %, reflecting
fundamental engineering constraints. Land-use concentration showed 78 %
of basins within cultivated lands and 18 percent in grasslands, with
minimal presence in natural vegetation areas (3 %), reflecting
deliberate agricultural intensification and land-use transformation.

3.3. The Vegetation Index and Precipitation Paradox in Bab Berred

NDVI\_~March-Jul~ analysis for Bab Berred revealed a distinctive
temporal pattern: slight increase from 1998 to 2012 (*Sen's slope 0.0025
per year*), followed by pronounced acceleration from 2013 onward. A
significant homogeneity break was detected in 2012 using trend analysis
(*p-value = 0.00000434, p \< 0.05; tau = 0.501*), indicating a critical
shift in vegetation dynamics. Peak NDVI amplitudes increased most
dramatically during the cannabis growing season (May-August), suggesting
enhanced irrigation-enabled vegetation productivity (Figure 1-a).

In stark contrast, annual precipitation exhibited a complex pattern of
declining trend with pronounced interannual variability. Analysis of
mean annual precipitation from 1985 to 2024 reveals a progressive
decline in precipitation levels over time, with Mann-Kendall testing
indicating a significant negative trend (*tau = -0.1376*) (Figure 1-b).
However, substantial year-to-year variability masks this overall
decline: exceptionally wet years (1996, 2010, 2018) exceeded 850 mm,
while severely dry years (1995, 1998, 2005, 2015, 2020) fell below 410
mm. Most dramatically, 2023 recorded the lowest precipitation on record
at approximately 300 mm. This declining precipitation trajectory,
combined with high interannual variability, intensified hydrological
pressure on agriculture and motivated continued expansion of artificial
water storage infrastructure as a drought-mitigation strategy. The
divergence between declining precipitation (particularly acute after
2014) and accelerating vegetation index improvement (post-2012)
constitutes the study's most significant finding, demonstrating that
irrigation infrastructure fundamentally compensates for natural water
deficit and sustains vegetation productivity despite increasing climatic
aridification.

3.4. Ecological Interpretation and Sustainability Implications

NDVI improvement reflects enhanced biomass productivity but does not
necessarily indicate ecosystem health or biodiversity status
improvement. The improved NDVI signal increasingly derives from
irrigated cannabis fields during peak growth season (March to July), a
high-biomass monoculture crop. During these peak months, cannabis NDVI
values exceed 0.75, among the highest NDVI values across agricultural
systems globally due to cannabis cultivation intensity and dense canopy
development. This "green signal" paradoxically masks simultaneous
ecological degradation evident in land-use change analysis. Natural
areas (grassland and rangeland) within Bab Berred declined and
cultivated agricultural area expanded (Figure 1). This land-use
transformation reveals the true territorial dynamic: natural ecosystems
regression and conversion to intensive irrigated cannabis cultivation,
not ecosystem-wide improvement.

Landscape fragmentation accompanying basin infrastructure and land
conversion represents an underappreciated ecological consequence (Figure
2). Natural spaces became increasingly fragmented as cannabis
cultivation expanded through intervening grasslands and cleared areas,
disrupting wildlife habitat connectivity and reducing sanctuary area for
endemic Mediterranean species. The Rif Mountains harbor important
biodiversity, including endemic plant species; several occur in
restricted refugia within or adjacent to Bab Berred. Fragmentation of
these natural spaces directly threatens species persistence and
ecosystem functionality.

The condition of the aquifer represents another critical sustainability
concern in Bab Berred. Actual water extraction from artificial basins
and irrigation systems exceeds the annual renewable recharge capacity of
local aquifers, causing progressive piezometric decline and threatening
long-term water security \[9\]. This unsustainable extraction pattern,
enabled by infrastructure expansion, is incompatible with hydrological
system recovery, particularly given increasing precipitation deficits
and climate-induced aridification. The cumulative impacts of numerous
small reservoirs on local hydrology create complex pressure dynamics
that may trigger unintended consequences for ecosystem functioning and
agricultural sustainability \[13\].

These results illuminate a critical rural adaptation paradox. Faced with
climate aridification and economic opportunity from cannabis
cultivation, Bab Berred communities deployed infrastructural innovation
enabling agricultural intensification. This represents a rational
short-term strategy. Basin construction stabilizes farming livelihoods,
buffers against drought risk, and enables cultivation of high-value
cannabis crops generating substantial household income in a historically
impoverished rural territory \[14\]. This case study resonates broadly
across Mediterranean and semi-arid territories where similar patterns of
infrastructure-intensive agricultural transformation occur \[15\]. The
apparent NDVI improvement paradox, productivity gains masking ecological
deterioration, likely characterizes many such regions globally yet
remains underappreciated in remote sensing-based environmental
monitoring focused on NDVI trends.

3.5. Governance and Territorial Management Implications

Sustainable territorial futures in Bab Berred require integrated
governance frameworks addressing water security, ecological
preservation, and livelihood sustainability simultaneously. The current
situation reflects the absence of coordinated management: individual
cultivators optimize household economic advantage through basin
construction, generating individually rational but collectively
unsustainable decisions (aquifer depletion, landscape fragmentation).
Necessary reforms include: hydrological assessments imposing sustainable
extraction ceilings, protection of natural habitats, agricultural
diversification, improved water efficiency, and land-use planning
integrating conservation with agricultural intensification. In this
context, remote sensing proves essential for this governance approach:
vegetation indices alone suggest environmental improvement, but
integrated analysis with precipitation and land-use data reveals
underlying sustainability challenges. This perspective applies globally
to similar Mediterranean regions.

4\. Conclusion

Artificial water basin proliferation in Bab Berred rural municipality
represents a geographically widespread rural adaptation strategy to
climate variability and economic opportunity from cannabis cultivation.
Remote sensing analysis reveals rapid infrastructure deployment enabling
agricultural intensification and maintained productivity despite
declining precipitation. However, the apparent vegetation improvement
paradox masks critical sustainability challenges: potential groundwater
overexploitation, ecosystem transformation, and biodiversity loss. These
results underscore the necessity of integrated territorial governance
transcending infrastructure investment alone. Sustainable rural futures
require simultaneous attention to hydrological limits, ecosystem
integrity, and livelihood resilience, particularly as climate change
intensifies water scarcity pressures across Mediterranean mountain
regions.
