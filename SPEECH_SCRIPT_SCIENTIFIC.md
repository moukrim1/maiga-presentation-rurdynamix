## Spatio-Temporal Analysis of Artificial Basins Dynamics in Bab Berred

**Duration**: 10 minutes
**Audience**: Researchers, scholars, university faculty

## SLIDE 1 - TITLE (0:30)

**[Start with clear, confident introduction]**

"Good morning. My name is Said Moukrim, and I am presenting research conducted with Ousmane Samba MAÏGA and colleagues from Mohammed-V University in Rabat, Faculty of Sciences.

Today I present a study on rural territorial transformation in Mediterranean mountain regions, specifically examining the spatio-temporal dynamics of artificial water basins in Bab Berred, in the central Moroccan Rif. This research addresses critical questions about infrastructure adaptation, land-use change, and ecosystem sustainability in contexts of climate variability and agricultural intensification.

Thank you for your attention. Let me begin with the hydrological context that motivates this research."

## SLIDE 2 - HYDROLOGICAL CONTEXT (0:50)

**[Establish scientific framing]**

"Mediterranean mountain regions face increasing hydroclimatic constraints. In the Rif Mountains specifically, we observe a progressive precipitation deficit, alongside intensifying seasonal water stress that undermines traditional rainfed agricultural systems.

In response to these hydroclimatic challenges, rural communities have deployed artificial basins as infrastructure-based drought-mitigation strategy. These structures function to harvest water from both surface runoff and groundwater resources during periods of availability, storing this water for release during dry periods when agricultural demand exceeds natural supply.

This adaptation represents a rational response to immediate water scarcity. However, the sustainability implications of large-scale basin proliferation remain poorly understood. Our research investigates the spatio-temporal dynamics of this infrastructure expansion, examines its relationship to broader agricultural intensification processes, and evaluates its environmental consequences.

The fundamental questions guiding this work are: What drives the spatial and temporal patterns of basin construction? How does infrastructure-mediated water availability alter vegetation dynamics? And critically, is current extraction rate sustainable relative to aquifer recharge capacity?"

---

## SLIDE 3 - AGRICULTURAL INTENSIFICATION (0:45)

**[Explain market-driven transformation, not legalization]**

"The expansion of artificial basins coincides with a fundamental transformation in agricultural practice: the introduction of modern hybrid cannabis varieties beginning in the early 2010s. This transition is driven by international market demand, not by legal frameworks. I emphasize this distinction because understanding causality is essential for policy evaluation.

The productivity differential between traditional and modern varieties is substantial: local kif cultivation yields approximately 15 kilograms of hashish equivalent per hectare through traditional methods, whereas modern hybrid varieties yield approximately 70 kilograms per hectare. This four-to-five-fold productivity increase explains rapid farmer adoption despite significant increases in input requirements, particularly water.

The 2021 legalization, Law 13-21, created a regulatory framework enabling official cultivation channels. However, the actual cultivated area substantially exceeds officially authorized area, indicating that international market demand has been the dominant factor shaping expansion dynamics throughout the period examined.

The hydrological consequence is direct: hybrid varieties require substantially greater water availability than rainfed systems can provide. This requirement fundamentally drove the transition to irrigated agriculture and, consequently, the proliferation of artificial water storage infrastructure we document in this analysis."

---

## SLIDE 4 - STUDY AREA (0:45)

**[Provide geographic and climatic context]**

"Our study site is Bab Berred, a rural municipality in Chefchaouen Province in the central Rif Mountains of northern Morocco. The municipality encompasses 172.24 square kilometers, with a resident population of approximately 24,742 people, almost entirely dependent on agriculture for livelihood.

Geographically, Bab Berred is characterized by pronounced topographic complexity: elevation ranges from 650 to 2,100 meters, with average slope gradient of approximately 80 percent, indicating extremely steep terrain. This topographic context creates both constraints and opportunities for water management.

Climatically, the region experiences Mediterranean mountain climate, characterized by mean annual precipitation exceeding 700 millimeters, though with pronounced seasonal concentration during winter months (October through April). Summer periods (May through September) are hot and dry, creating marked seasonal water stress. Mean annual temperature is approximately 18.6 degrees Celsius, with June mean temperature of 24.6 degrees Celsius.

This ecological and climatic context—geographically marginal, hydrologically constrained, economically dependent on agriculture—creates the conditions within which infrastructure-intensive adaptation strategies emerge."

## SLIDE 5 - METHODOLOGY (1:00)

**[Explain data sources and analytical approach]**

"Our analysis integrates multiple data streams through a spatiotemporal analytical framework.

For remote sensing analysis, we employed Landsat 5, 7, and 8 satellite imagery at 30-meter resolution covering the period 1998 to 2025, accessed through the Google Earth Engine platform. This extensive temporal archive enables vegetation index calculation and analysis of long-term trends. Historical imagery from Google Earth Pro and Esri World Imagery Wayback archive enabled manual basin digitization and temporal reconstruction of basin construction sequence from 2010 onwards.

Climate data consisted of annual precipitation time series from 1985 to 2025, derived from satellite precipitation products. We applied Mann-Kendall non-parametric trend analysis to detect monotonic trends in precipitation, an approach appropriate for non-normal distributions and irregular sampling intervals.

Land-cover classification employed the ESA WorldCover 2021 product, which provides standardized global classification at 10-meter resolution. Seven land-cover classes were identified within the Bab Berred study area: tree cover, shrubland, grassland, cultivated areas, built-up zones, sparse vegetation, and bare soil.

Spatial analysis was conducted using QGIS for vector and raster processing. We characterized basin spatio-temporal dynamics, analyzed basin distribution relative to topographic variables and land-use categories, and computed basin morphometric properties including surface area, estimated depth, and storage volume.

This integrated methodological approach enables comprehensive assessment of infrastructure dynamics, ecosystem response, and sustainability implications."

## SLIDE 6 - BASIN PROLIFERATION (1:00)

**[Present key finding on basin growth]**

"Basin inventory for Bab Berred reveals extraordinary proliferation of artificial water storage infrastructure. The analysis identified approximately 28 basins in 2010 and 2,044 basins by 2025. This represents a 73-fold expansion in 15 years.

The aggregate storage capacity expanded correspondingly from approximately 50,000 cubic meters in 2010 to approximately 4.3 million cubic meters in 2025—an 85-fold increase. This magnitude of change represents fundamentally altered hydrology at the territorial scale.

Morphometric analysis reveals a striking miniaturization trend. The mean basin size declined from 1,260 square meters in 2010 to 812 square meters in 2025. By 2025, 90 percent of all basins occupied surface area less than 1,500 square meters. This miniaturization pattern reflects resource constraints of economically limited rural communities. Rather than constructing fewer large reservoirs requiring substantial capital investment, farmers built numerous modest basins incrementally as capital became available.

The growth trajectory itself warrants attention. Expansion remained gradual from 2010 through 2014, then accelerated substantially from 2015 onwards, with particularly intense construction in 2022 to 2025. The 2024-2025 period alone recorded 372 newly constructed basins, indicating sustained infrastructure investment despite questions about long-term viability.

This proliferation represents massive hydrological transformation of the territory and raises fundamental questions about sustainability."

---

## SLIDE 7 - SPATIAL DISTRIBUTION (1:00)

**[Explain geographic patterns in basin location]**

"Basin distribution throughout Bab Berred demonstrates clear patterns reflecting adaptation to biophysical constraints rather than evidence of centralized territorial planning.

Altitude analysis reveals basin concentration between 800 and 1,500 meters elevation. This distribution reflects optimal balance between irrigation demand intensity and water source proximity. Basins avoid very high elevations, where construction becomes difficult and costly, and very low elevations characterized by high aridity and distance from reliable water sources.

Slope preference is pronounced: 71 percent of all basins occupy slopes between 5 and 15 percent gradient. Basins are virtually absent on slopes exceeding 25 percent, reflecting fundamental engineering constraints. Gravity-fed irrigation systems function optimally on moderate slopes; very steep slopes impose prohibitive construction costs relative to economic returns.

Land-use distribution shows 78 percent of basins within cultivated agricultural areas, 18 percent in grasslands, and only 3 percent in natural vegetation zones. This distribution indicates deliberate placement within production zones rather than random distribution. Basin construction concentrates in areas of active agricultural intensification, particularly cannabis cultivation.

This spatial analysis demonstrates that basin location responds to biophysical and economic logic, with no evidence of coordinated territorial planning. Individual farmers optimize household advantage through basin placement; collective spatial outcomes reflect aggregation of individual decisions within environmental constraints."

---

## SLIDE 8 - VEGETATION INDEX EVOLUTION (1:00)

**[Present NDVI trends]**

"We analyzed vegetation index dynamics using normalized difference vegetation index calculated from Landsat surface reflectance data, restricting analysis to the March through July period to focus on peak cannabis cultivation and growth season and to minimize seasonal effects.

The temporal pattern reveals two distinct periods. From 1998 through 2012, vegetation index values increased gradually, with Sen's slope of approximately 0.0025 per year. This period shows relatively modest and stable change.

The transition point occurs in 2012. Beginning in 2013 and continuing through 2025, we observe marked acceleration in vegetation index values. Statistical homogeneity break analysis detected the 2012 inflection point with extremely high significance: p-value of 4.34 times 10 to the negative sixth power, with Mann-Kendall tau of 0.501, indicating strong positive trend.

Seasonal analysis reveals that peak NDVI amplitudes occur during the cannabis growing season, specifically May through August. This temporal correspondence suggests that the vegetation index signal derives primarily from irrigated cannabis cultivation rather than from broader ecosystem recovery.

The critical finding is temporal correspondence between basin proliferation onset (2012-2013) and vegetation index inflection point (2012). This synchronicity suggests that infrastructure-based water availability fundamentally alters vegetation productivity dynamics in the region."

---

## SLIDE 9 - PRECIPITATION TRENDS (1:00)

**[Present precipitation decline]**

"Annual precipitation analysis over the 1985-2024 period reveals a significant negative trend. Mann-Kendall test yields tau value of negative 0.1376, with statistical significance of p less than 0.05, confirming that precipitation decline is a genuine long-term trend rather than random variability.

However, interannual variability is substantial and masks this underlying trend. Exceptionally wet years—1996, 2010, 2018—exceeded 850 millimeters precipitation. Severely dry years—1995, 1998, 2005, 2015, 2020—recorded less than 410 millimeters. This high variability creates extreme planning uncertainty for agricultural communities.

The most extreme event in the record is 2023, which recorded approximately 300 millimeters, the lowest precipitation on record for the study period. Such extreme events, if they represent emerging climatic patterns rather than isolated anomalies, would create severe agricultural stress.

The divergence between this long-term precipitation decline and the simultaneous improvement in vegetation index values represents the study's most significant finding. While precipitation generally decreased, vegetation productivity improved markedly. This apparent paradox requires explanation: What mechanism sustains vegetation productivity when natural water availability diminishes?"

---

## SLIDE 10 - PARADOX DISCUSSION (1:00)

**[Explain the apparent paradox]**

"The paradox resolves through understanding how infrastructure mediates water availability. Declining precipitation drives necessity for water storage. Artificial basins harvest and store water from periods of availability, releasing it during scarcity. This infrastructure-based system sustains vegetation productivity despite precipitation deficit.

However, the nature of this vegetation productivity requires critical scrutiny. NDVI measures biomass accumulation but does not indicate ecosystem health, biodiversity status, or ecological integrity. The high NDVI signal (exceeding 0.75, among the highest of any global agricultural system) derives entirely from irrigated cannabis monoculture.

Land-use change analysis reveals the ecological reality contradicting the remote sensing signal. Natural vegetation areas—grasslands and rangelands—demonstrate regression. Cultivated agricultural area expands correspondingly. The territorial dynamic is ecosystem conversion, not ecosystem recovery. Natural areas become agricultural areas.

The critical implication: Apparent vegetation improvement masks simultaneous ecological degradation. Remote sensing vegetation indices alone provide fundamentally inadequate basis for sustainability assessment. The high NDVI signal is real but misleading.

Furthermore, the water extraction enabling this apparent improvement proceeds at rates exceeding annual groundwater recharge capacity. We observe progressive piezometric decline—water table lowering—indicating aquifer overexploitation. The short-term productivity gains achieved through infrastructure development may generate long-term water security crisis through unsustainable extraction dynamics."

---

## SLIDE 11 - CUMULATIVE IMPACTS AND GOVERNANCE (1:00)

**[Address ecosystem and governance implications]**

"The cumulative impacts of basin proliferation extend beyond hydrological concerns to encompass ecosystem degradation with particular severity for biodiversity.

Landscape fragmentation accompanies basin infrastructure and land-use conversion. Basin construction, agricultural expansion, and access roads fragment natural habitat into increasingly isolated patches. This fragmentation disrupts wildlife habitat connectivity and reduces sanctuary area for endemic Mediterranean species. The Rif Mountains harbor important biodiversity, including plant species occurring in restricted geographic refugia. Several such species occur within or adjacent to Bab Berred. Habitat fragmentation from basin infrastructure and agricultural conversion directly threatens species persistence.

Concurrently, the hydrological system experiences stress from cumulative impacts. Two thousand basins, while individually modest, create complex collective pressure on local hydrological dynamics. Groundwater extraction exceeds annual recharge capacity. Progressive piezometric decline threatens long-term water availability. These dynamics create a fundamental tension: infrastructure solves short-term drought risk while creating long-term depletion risk.

This dynamic represents an adaptation paradox. Individual farmers rationally construct basins, responding to immediate water scarcity and economic opportunity. Collectively, however, these individual decisions generate unsustainable aquifer extraction—a tragedy of the commons dynamic.

Governance frameworks addressing this paradox must simultaneously achieve three objectives: ensure hydrological sustainability through extraction ceilings based on recharge capacity assessment; protect remaining natural ecosystems and endemic species habitat; and maintain livelihood resilience for rural communities. Infrastructure investment alone cannot substitute for comprehensive integrated resource management addressing coupled natural-human systems."

---

## SLIDE 12 - CONCLUSION (0:45)

**[Synthesize findings and frame global implications]**

"This research documents rapid spatio-temporal proliferation of artificial basins—from 28 to 2,044 structures over 15 years—that fundamentally transforms territorial hydrology and vegetation dynamics. Infrastructure enables continued agricultural productivity despite declining precipitation. However, this apparent vegetation improvement masks ecosystem transformation and aquifer overexploitation that threaten long-term sustainability.

The findings extend beyond Bab Berred's specific context. Infrastructure-intensive agricultural transformation characterizes contemporary adaptation processes across Mediterranean regions, Central Asia, and global semi-arid territories. Similar patterns likely occur in many regions, often unrecognized because remote sensing-based environmental monitoring frequently misidentifies ecological status when NDVI trends alone are considered.

We demonstrate that integrated analysis incorporating multiple data streams—vegetation indices, precipitation trends, land-use change, and hydrological assessment—is essential for accurate sustainability characterization. NDVI improvement cannot be interpreted as ecological improvement without consideration of broader ecosystem context.

Sustainable rural futures in regions like Bab Berred and globally require simultaneous attention to three dimensions: hydrological limits defined by renewable resource capacity, ecosystem integrity including biodiversity conservation, and livelihood resilience for dependent communities. These objectives compete but are not irreconcilable. Their reconciliation requires governance frameworks integrating scientific knowledge, local expertise, and institutional coordination across administrative scales.

As climate change intensifies water scarcity pressures across Mediterranean and semi-arid regions, the urgency of such integrated governance intensifies. Infrastructure innovation is necessary but insufficient. This case study from the Moroccan Rif illuminates sustainability challenges and adaptation paradoxes that characterize contemporary rural transformation globally.

Thank you for your attention. I welcome your questions and discussion."
